Key points

    Sources

    • United States, China, European Union: population by age. United Nations, World Population Prospects 2024 (counts to 2023, medium projection after; the EU is the sum of its 27 members). Yearly indicators: World Bank. US recessions: National Bureau of Economic Research.
    • Canada: population by age. Statistics Canada tables 17-10-0005-01 (1990 to 2026) and 17-10-0057-01 (projection, scenario M1, 2027 to 2050).
    • Life expectancy, mothers' age, fertility by age. Statistics Canada tables 13-10-0837-01, 13-10-0417-01 and 13-10-0418-01.
    • In school; living alone or with children; born outside Canada. Statistics Canada tables 37-10-0101-01, 98-10-0134-01 (2011, 2016, 2021 censuses) and 98-10-0347-01 (2021 census).
    • Born the same year. A widely known person from that place, shown only for birth years that have one; chosen from the most widely covered people on Wikidata.
    • Generations. Statistics Canada's 2021 Census names for Canada; Pew Research Center's for the United States (borrowed for the EU, which has none of its own); decade of birth for China.
    • Births per woman. Our World in Data long-run fertility series to 1990; Statistics Canada 13-10-0418-01 after.
    • Immigration. Statistics Canada table 17-10-0008-01.
    • Economic growth. Maddison Project Database (via Our World in Data) to 1960; World Bank after.
    • Recessions. C.D. Howe Institute Business Cycle Council; Cross and Bergevin, 2012.
    • Starting work in a recession. Oreopoulos, von Wachter and Heisz, "The Short- and Long-Term Career Effects of Graduating in a Recession", 2012.
    • Workers per non-worker. People in work for each person who is not, of any age. Each age group's population is multiplied by the share of that group in work: Statistics Canada table 14-10-0327-01 for Canada (five-year age groups); International Labour Organization modelled estimates elsewhere (four age groups, participation less unemployment). Years after 2025 hold the 2025 rates, so only the mix of ages changes. Prime age means 25 to 54.
    • Unemployment, income, education. Statistics Canada tables 14-10-0327-01, 11-10-0239-01 and 14-10-0118-01.
    • Tuition. Statistics Canada tables 37-10-0150-01 and 37-10-0045-01, adjusted with the Consumer Price Index (18-10-0005-01).
    • House prices. Bank for International Settlements, real residential property prices, Canada.
    • Spending on housing and food. Statistics Canada table 11-10-0222-01, as a share of household consumption.
    • Marriage and living with parents. Statistics Canada table 39-10-0056-01; The Daily, 13 July 2022 (census years, joined by straight lines).
